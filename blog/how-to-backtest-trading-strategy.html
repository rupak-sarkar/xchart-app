📌 What is Backtesting?

Backtesting is the process of testing a trading strategy on historical data to see how it would have performed in the past. Instead of risking real money on an untested idea, you run it against years of actual market data and measure the results.

Think of it like a flight simulator for traders — you experience the market without the risk.

💡 Why it matters: Studies show that 90% of retail traders in India lose money. The #1 reason? They trade on tips, emotions, and untested ideas. Backtesting removes the guesswork. If your strategy doesn't work on 3 years of historical data, it probably won't work tomorrow.

⚠️ 6 Common Backtesting Mistakes

  1. Over-fitting (Too Many Indicators)
    Using 8-10 indicators makes your strategy look amazing on historical data but fail in live trading. Stick to 2-4 indicators maximum. More isn't better.
  2. No Stop Loss
    Every professional trader uses a stop loss. If your backtest doesn't include one, the results are meaningless. A strategy with 80% win rate but no SL can still blow up your account on the 20%.
  3. Ignoring Slippage & Fees
    Backtests assume perfect execution at closing prices. In reality, you'll face slippage (0.1-0.5%), brokerage (₹20/trade on discount brokers), and STT/taxes. A strategy with PF 1.1 might actually lose money after costs.
  4. Testing Only on Winners
    Don't just backtest RELIANCE and TCS. Test on stocks that went sideways or down too. A good strategy should work across different market conditions.
  5. Too Short a Period
    Testing on 3 months of data is meaningless. Use at least 1 year (xchart.in provides 3 years). Your strategy should survive bull markets, bear markets, and sideways markets.
  6. Curve-Fitting to Past Data
    If you keep tweaking parameters until the backtest looks perfect, you've probably curve-fitted to the past. A robust strategy should work with reasonable parameter ranges, not one magic number.
💡 Pro tip: The best way to avoid over-fitting is to test your strategy on a stock you DIDN'T optimize it for. If your RSI strategy works on RELIANCE, test it on INFY, TCS, and HDFC too. If it only works on one stock, it's curve-fitted.
